##### 2016.05.10

We approached the Suez Canal—it was my first transit of it. Instead of the usual external anchorage, they moored us inside the port by a buoy with two anchors.  
A buoy is a mooring device—a concrete anchor secured in the seabed with a float attached by a chain to the anchor.  
Since the vessel was Heavy Lift class (capable of carrying oversized cargo), it was equipped with its own cranes capable of lifting 150 tons. So taking on the Suez mooring boats was a piece of cake.  
We also had some minor issues: various locals tried to come aboard to sell all sorts of junk to the sailors.  
In the photos below, you can see the mooring boat operation and the special quarantine lights—because we failed to switch them off, we were fined 9 packs of cigarettes. In the accommodation superstructure, all entrances and exits were closed except for one—next to which we cadets stood watch so that the local natives could not get inside.

##### 2016.06.10

After leaving all the cargo at previous ports, we arrived for loading. Since we were a Heavy Lift vessel, we were assigned to deliver 300 tons of generators to the UAE.  
The process of loading such heavy cargo is done a bit unusually:  
Firstly, two cranes operate in tandem.  
Secondly, a special high-speed ballast system is used to first heel the ship toward the shore, secure the cargo, and then heel the ship in the opposite direction; this is done because the force required to lift the cargo off the ground is greatest at that point and could even damage such powerful cranes.
