##### 2018.01.03 17:45

Hooray!! We’ve been confirmed that the cargo is **not** bound for Iraq but for Algeria, so by the end of February I should already be home^^

##### 2018.01.04 17:45

They sent us a letter saying we have to come alongside the dock tomorrow to load. On one hand, I don’t like ports, but on the other—if we arrive earlier, we’ll leave earlier and head to Algeria, and then home^^

##### 2018.01.05

###### 15:15

They sent tickets for the crew members who are leaving. They were so hoping to fly, but no—they’re in for a 40-hour “adventure” by bus from St. Pete to Odessa.

###### 18:00

Of course the Chief Officer decided to buy himself a plane ticket—why subject his body to a bus ride? 😅

###### 20:30

The route seems to have changed again: now it’s St. Pete → Iraq → Iraq → Egypt → Tunisia → UAE → Poland → St. Pete. That’s probably over four months… Wow. Goodbye, sanity.

##### 2018.01.06 02:10

The only hope is a crew change in the Suez Canal, whether for better or worse.

##### 2018.01.07 16:00

Surprisingly (really?), the old Chief Officer left, and it’s easier to breathe now. The nervous twitch hasn’t gone away yet, but time will help 😅. We’ll see what happens at sea, but for now the new Chief Officer is just great.

**Upd.** While in port in St. Petersburg (I don’t recall the exact date), one of the cadets who was standing watch at the gangway (the only place where people can embark or disembark—everyone must be logged and reported to the officers if authorities, shippers, or anyone else comes aboard) got drunk with someone else. I was standing cargo watch in the hold and couldn’t keep an eye on him, so he started insulting the new Chief Officer and almost got into a fight. They wanted to off-sign him, but he has a solid “backer” in the company. When I went up to the bridge, my heart sank to my feet. Even the Captain was surprised and offered me whiskey to warm up. I was just terrified they’d blame me for not watching that troublemaker.

##### 2018.01.09

###### 00:00

According to the passage plan, we should arrive in Iraq on March 10th (the odd thing is that it takes a month to get there—why such timing?), and by then I’ll have six months at sea. So there are two options: a crew change **before** the Suez or **after**. The ship is in the process of loading, which will finish soon, and then we’ll leave St. Petersburg.

###### 17:30

That’s it—an hour from now we leave port. Finally, hooray!

##### 2018.01.15 19:45

We’re anchored in the safe harbor of Torquay in Tor Bay, due to a terrible storm in the Bay of Biscay. As DPA said, we could stay here a week or a month—what matters is the safety of the ship, the cargo, and the crew.  
Despite that, we still got battered by Force 8 winds between the Dover Strait and this harbor.  
Psychologically, work has become many times easier, and the new Chief Officer is much more experienced and doesn’t give pointless orders. Morale on the ship has improved dramatically, though half the crew still wants to go home.  
By the way, today is exactly four months since we left port.  
Yesterday, for the first time in three months, we had a proper meal (the cook was replaced three months ago). One of the seamen made shawarma for everyone, and it was incredibly delicious. Considering how bad our current cook’s food is, I think anything would taste amazing 😅.  
That’s all for now—report over.

Because of extremely strong winds reaching 40–50 knots, last night we “dragged” so the ship had to be secured on two anchors, each with eight 27.5-meter chain stubs. Fortunately, the wind is only 30–35 knots now, but the forecast says it will worsen again :(  
Other than that, everything’s fine. :)

##### 2018.01.19

###### 19:50

In a couple of hours our ship will weigh anchor and head into the Bay of Biscay to take its punishment. The storm has eased a bit, but there’s still no pleasure in it. I hope everything goes well. The stay in England was pretty decent, aside from resetting anchors, so it’s time to get back to work.

###### 22:30

That’s it—we weighed anchor. Farewell, friends, until next time.

##### 2018.01.21

For the second day, we’ve been steaming through storms in the Bay of Biscay. Overall, the weather isn’t so bad—but that’s for a regular ship. Our flat-bottom riverboat gets pitched wildly. Speed is not bad—it has picked up—but we can’t outrun the next storm, so we’ll be partying until Gibraltar. The rolling is already driving us crazy: we haven’t eaten a proper meal or slept for a day and a half, but that’s a minor inconvenience—worse things have happened. On my last contract, we were tossed around in the Indian Ocean for two weeks.

##### 2018.01.22

This morning we left the Bay of Biscay, and the ship stopped rolling so much—only residual 10–15 degrees, which is nothing after what we just endured.  
The crew’s state is miserable—everyone except the newly arrived Chief Officer, the 3rd Engineer, and the seamen want to get off as soon as possible. But it’s still too early to expect that before the beginning of April. The cook has started cooking “better,” apparently he found a bolt bigger than the one he was using before.  
Other than that, everything’s going according to plan :)

##### 2018.01.24

Good day! We passed through the Strait of Gibraltar and are heading toward Greece to take on fuel. Soon we’ll be in the Suez Canal, then we’ll take on pirate security and proceed through the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden into the Persian Gulf to Iraq, port of Umm-Qasr, where I’ve been once before. Then we’ll load again in Egypt, and after a few days we’ll load once more in Saudi Arabia bound for Tunisia. The only hope is that after that our “masters” will deign to let their faithful slaves go home.  
Otherwise, everything is normal. I’ve been checking everything after the storm and correcting some deficiencies. Of course, the new Chief Officer is far better than the old one, but it doesn’t change the fact that I still lack the experience I’d like to have—but I’m trying.

##### 2018.01.25

Since yesterday I’ve been struck by a wave of apathy—really don’t want to do anything. Of course I’m fighting it, but it’s still rather depressing.

##### 2018.01.27

Right now we’re near Malta :)  
That port in Egypt where we’ll unload the cargo from Iraq is Suez, and there’s a possibility of going home much earlier than mid-April, which is very pleasing.

##### 2018.01.28

And now something amusing happened: while the 3rd Engineer and I were eating instant noodles (after my night watch), the Captain came by and… made himself instant noodles 😅. Apparently he can’t stand our cook’s food either, just like the rest of the crew 😅😅😅.

##### 2018.01.28 21:00

Today the Chief Officer held a crew meeting about dissatisfaction with the cook, but no one spoke up officially or filed a complaint. So although the cook lost his bonus, he got off easy.  
In eight hours we should be approaching Kali Limenes, Crete, Greece, to bunker the ship. I’ve started feeling much better mentally—today was surprisingly pleasant.  
In general, food quality has worsened because of the terrible products in Russia :( In Egypt we’ll take on some extra provisions, and it should get easier and tastier.

##### 2018.01.29 10:20

That’s it—we’re leaving. Until next time, friends^^

##### 2018.02.02 Evening Watch

Yesterday we changed our clocks to UTC+3, and today the crew held a barbecue on board (or “Shashlik Drill” 😂). The meat turned out divinely delicious. There were also sandwiches with red caviar, which was supposed to be for New Year but arrived too late, plus vegetables, salads, potatoes with lard—it was a nice morale boost for the crew, and it was all in honor of the Captain’s daughter’s birth.  
Today we’ll take on security for pirate protection in a dangerous area and continue on our way.  
The agent sent us a letter with arrival documents, and it said that this ship will most likely undergo PSC (Port State Control)—what a booby trap to drop at the end of the contract.  
I hope we pass it as smoothly as usual in these countries.  
That’s the current situation :)

##### 2018.02.05

We’re sailing through the pirate zone, having passed Bab-el-Mandeb, and we’re heading through the Gulf of Aden along the international recommended transit corridor (IRTC). In the last couple of days, I’ve been homesick again. I started watching the anime “The Disappearance of Yuki Nagato.” It’s nothing extraordinary except for the soundtracks—classic pieces like “Claire de Lune” that I really love. We’re working bit by bit, fixing issues wherever we find them.

##### 2018.02.13

We passed that pirate-infested area, as usual without incident, and sailed through the Persian Gulf with its numerous fishing boats. It’s surprisingly cool here—around +15°C at night. Right now we’re in port. Although the type of cargo we brought normally gets unchained by stevedores, this time **we** had to do it ourselves :(, and when we finish unloading we’ll immediately have to reload and secure cargo again. Hopes for crew change in Suez are fading day by day. We can only hope that after passing Suez we’ll head straight to Ust-Luga or St. Pete.

##### 2018.02.18

We’re anchored at Fujairah, UAE, and soon we’ll pick up security again. We hope to go to Suez right away, but everyone is trying to come up with some “additional load” that will delay our return home. But I’m already six months in, so I’ve calmed down—at least there’s no more depression from wanting to go home, hehe. You could say I just don’t care anymore, hehehe.

##### 2018.02.25

We passed the dangerous pirate area and are approaching the unloading port—Suez/Adabiya. This morning the security team left the ship. One of the guards has been with us all four times we passed that zone and has become almost one of us. By the way, they’re all Russian-speaking, so there was no communication problem :) Morale has been good lately—like they say back home, it feels “homely.” I hope the last month flies by as quickly as the previous ones.  
Of course, there’s a chance we’ll get an extra load after Suez, but we can pray to all the gods that it doesn’t happen, hehehe. In the Red Sea, even in February, it’s up to +30°C, so we’re surviving under air conditioners and fans (so, envy us 😂😂😂). And when I get home, it will be warming up as well, so the only “winter” I saw this year was a week of snow in St. Pete at New Year.  
Funny enough, this Chief Officer also has quirks—he’s not as much of a jerk as the last one, but he still has “cockroaches,” hehe. Sometimes because of him we have to do some stupid, pointless work, or redo something that took days because he came up with a different idea, hehe. But whatever—what’s left to do?

##### 2018.02.27

Well, today was full of bad and not-so-bad news.  
First, the gods remained deaf to our prayers, and we have **another** load from England back to England, which means a PSC under the Paris MOU: we had three detentions last July. If you know, you know :(  
Second, someone still reported the cook to the company, and now they’re dismissing him for professional unsuitability right here in Suez.  
That’s about all the news.

##### 2018.02.28

It turned out—it’s logical—that the cook will be dismissed in the “next convenient port,” which is not Suez. After all, we’ll be docking there today or tomorrow. Technically, he still has a chance to correct things; I hope he takes it and that we start eating like normal people again.
