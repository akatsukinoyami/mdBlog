## Specifications

Not that I haven’t written about these on every second site on the internet, but here’s a brief reminder.

#### SoC

The Steam Deck is based on a custom AMD chip with 4 cores / 8 threads, integrated Vega 8 graphics, and a 15W TDP.

#### RAM

16 GB of RAM operate in quad-channel mode and are also used for graphics.

#### Storage

My version has a 512 GB SSD; there are also 256 GB and 64 GB versions. The SSD is not soldered—it’s an M.2 2230 module and can be user-replaced (Valve does not officially endorse this).

There’s also a microSD card slot, so you can expand storage by up to an additional terabyte or keep several cards on hand.

#### Display

The 7" screen has a resolution of 1280×800 with a 16:10 aspect ratio. This resolution is more than enough for gaming at this diagonal, though the aspect ratio raises questions. Some games don’t handle it properly and display black bars, or render the game full-screen but limit the HUD to a 16:9 area. However, this isn’t a frequent problem. This screen is not top-tier quality and has some backlight bleed, but it’s still quite decent.

#### Battery

The 40 Wh battery is relatively large for a handheld device and is close in capacity to laptop batteries. However, when running AAA titles, power consumption can reach about 23 W.

#### Ports

The USB Type-C port can be used both for charging and for connecting peripherals, hubs, monitors, and external drives. There’s also a 3.5 mm jack for headphones/speakers.

#### Controls

Besides the standard console sticks, D-pad, triggers, and A, B, X, Y buttons, there are rear buttons similar to those on elite controllers. One of the top features is the haptic touchpads. The screen is also touch-sensitive.

There are dedicated Steam buttons—for store management and most shortcuts—and a “…” button that opens a menu where you can limit TDP, CPU frequency, and display refresh rate to save power.

## User Experience

#### Games

At the time of writing, I’ve had the console for a month. I’m almost done playing Cyberpunk 2077 on it, and I often play Hatsune Miku: Project DIVA Mega Mix+.

AAA titles generally run at around 30 FPS on medium–low settings, while less demanding games can reach up to 60 FPS.

#### Docked Mode

You can easily connect the console to a TV and use any wireless controller (DualShock / DualSense / Xbox Controller), effectively giving you PS4-level power.

Some simpler games can run at 1080p, but higher resolutions are not recommended.

#### Desktop Mode

In the shutdown menu you can find “Switch to Desktop Mode,” and when you select it, you’ll be greeted with… KDE!

:::1
![KDE interface on the console screen](+images/kde.png)
:::
Yes, SteamOS is based on Arch Linux with the KDE desktop environment. This is an open operating system that does not forbid you from doing whatever you want.

Epic Games Store and GOG through the Heroic Launcher? Easy.

Pirated games? Go ahead!

There’s also the huge AUR (Arch User Repository) library, and the `yay` CLI utility for installing packages from AUR is preinstalled. Although that’s mainly important for power users like me)

In short—there are no restrictions for the end user here compared to closed consoles from companies like Sony, Microsoft, and Nintendo.

#### Emulators

Yes, they’re here, and there are many, since RetroArch is available in the Steam Store. Although I personally didn’t like its interface.

Emulating very old consoles, including the Sony PSP, poses no issues at all.

Nintendo Switch games often run at better quality and higher frame rates than on the original console (just don’t tell anyone—Big N is very upset about this fact).

PS4 and PSVita emulators are still in progress, but a PS3 emulator is available, offering a huge catalog of classic games from its era.

#### Shortcuts

In Windows/Linux or macOS, we get used to “hotkey” combinations to speed up work. The console also has many such shortcuts that greatly simplify working with the device and make it more enjoyable.

## Conclusion

The console turned out to be quite good, and I really like it. Quality build, sufficient performance to replace a PS4, and form factor that lets you take it with you on trips. The battery is not outstanding, but if you play indie games on the road, you can stretch it to over 6 hours.

I can’t say it’s a “must-have,” but it definitely deserves every geek’s attention)
