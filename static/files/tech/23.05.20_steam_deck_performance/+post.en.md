## Games

Let’s move on to more important things—after all, games are why the Steam Deck is purchased.

I have two main scenarios for using the device: playing directly on the Steam Deck’s screen and connecting it to the TV. Usually, I don’t use the latter scenario for AAA titles, since I have a desktop PC, but I did run tests at 800p and 1080p. When playing at 1080p, I did not use FSR, in order to show the raw performance of the hardware. If desired, I can run tests with FSR too.

UPD: After some time, I also decided to check less demanding games on my 3440×1440 monitor—and in older projects, the Steam Deck performed quite well!

### Atomic Heart

This game is currently fully compatible with the Steam Deck, but the developers for some reason removed the graphics settings menu when playing in handheld mode. However, as before, the game is simply very well optimized. It’s noticeable that at 1080p the image is a bit blurry, which suggests it’s still rendering internally at 800p, and graphical detail has been heavily reduced to maintain framerate.

After attempting to run the game at 1440p, it becomes clear that the developers aggressively drop quality settings when increasing resolution.

### Cities: Skylines

You might think, how do you play a city-building simulator on a console without a keyboard and mouse? I also thought it was impossible, but this game fully reveals the power and necessity of the Steam Deck’s touchpads—without them, it’s hard even to browse. However, while it is possible to play, it’s far more comfortable with a keyboard and trackball.

Since the game is not as GPU-bound as it is CPU-bound, I let my city grow to 23k residents on my PC, then imported that save. The game shows almost equally low FPS at any resolution, hitting a CPU bottleneck. But with smaller city sizes, it can output a playable 30 FPS.

The hit from CD Projekt Red. At launch, it was unplayable on previous-gen consoles, but on the Steam Deck it has full compatibility. It runs excellently—I can’t say anything bad. The game never drops below 30 FPS in handheld mode, and when connected to a monitor, it outputs around 25 FPS without FSR.

### Euro Truck Simulator 2

I love this game for its meditative feel; it’s as if it puts you in a trance while you just steer. It’s also great for listening to audiobooks if you want to give your brain a rest. Plus, this game is available on almost any platform—even on MacBooks!

At 1080p, the game approaches 25 FPS, but I would recommend lowering resolution and using SteamOS’s built-in FSR rather than dropping graphic settings, since lowering settings can turn the world into a sprite-filled mess reminiscent of the ’90s. Even at 1440p, the Steam Deck remains quite stable on medium settings. However, FSR is advisable at that resolution.

### Hatsune Miku: Project DIVA Mega Mix+

This game has a special place in my heart. I first started playing it back in school on the PSP, where there were three entries (1, 2nd and Extend). Later, after college, I founded a small community of like-minded fans. I bought a PS Vita exclusively for this series, where there were three more entries (f, f2nd, and X), and eventually a PS4, where Sega released the arcade-to-console port Hatsune Miku: Project DIVA Future Tone.

The PC version, Mega Mix+, is remarkable—it’s a port of a Switch title, which was a port from PS4, which itself was a port from arcade machines.

The PC version runs very well on any hardware, so obviously you could hit 60 FPS on a Steam Deck even at 4K resolution.

Mega Mix+ is also very mod-friendly, even though Steam Workshop support isn’t enabled, allowing the community to add many custom songs.

### Hogwarts Legacy

The hit from Avalanche Studios that captured many hearts. The game is beautiful, interesting, and unique. Although the story takes place in the Harry Potter universe, it barely feels like it. However, optimization is lacking—on low settings at 800p it’s barely playable with frequent FPS drops. The only advice is to use FSR even in handheld mode, and don’t bother trying to play it on a monitor.

### Horizon: Zero Dawn

This was my first game on the PS4 and the first game I ever purchased officially. On PS4 it looked quite blurry, and I doubt it even ran at 1080p, so it was so much more enjoyable on PC where it transformed into a gorgeously detailed experience. Initially, it felt very laggy, but the default settings were on Ultra. When I dropped to minimum settings, the game ran at a console-standard 30 FPS at 1080p, and at 800p it even approaches 60 FPS.

### Nier: Automata

This game struck a deep chord in me. No other game, except maybe Cyberpunk, left such a lingering post-depressive aftertaste. And no, I mean that in a positive way. It truly is a masterpiece. Just the soundtrack alone gives me goosebumps.

Back in the day, I played a pirated version with a Russian translation. Now I have the official version, but it lacks translation. You can install a fan translation on the official version, but I haven’t bothered, since I probably won’t replay it.

At medium settings, FPS noticeably drops even at 800p, so it’s better to use Low settings and enjoy the unattainable 60 FPS on consoles.

### Stray

The game about a poor kitty in a post-apocalyptic underground world full of robots exploded onto the scene in 2022. The main thing to know is—you play as a cat! The gameplay isn’t outstanding, but you can scratch rugs, knock down a paint can from a rooftop, meow, or just lounge around. It’s not very demanding and, oddly enough, has the same framerate at both 800p and 1080p. However, at 1440p the game noticeably dips, barely balancing at 20 FPS on minimum settings.

### The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim

The unforgettable classic that many have purchased multiple times, since it’s hard to find a platform Skyrim hasn’t been on. We all remember Helgen and the gate entering Riverwood, so I won’t drag out the description. On Ultra settings, we get about 30 FPS at both 800p and 1080p, which is quite playable, but I’ll try to reach 60 FPS and report back.

On Low settings, you can get a solid 40 FPS at 1440p, although the game doesn’t understand or correctly render on 21:9 monitors, which is likely fixable with mods.

### Timberborn

An interesting society-building simulator about beavers in a post-apocalypse. You have to grow food, trees, and ensure that the beavers have water during droughts. You can also build factories that create golems, which in turn build more factories))

I found the game via a macOS torrent, but I got so into it that I bought it on Steam. Although the game is still in active development, constantly receiving new features and fixes (though I didn’t encounter many issues). The game adapts well to different screen resolutions and includes sliders for render resolution and UI scaling.
