I'll start from afar — the French game Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 opened up the world of turn-based JRPGs for me. So I picked up a bunch of ATLUS titles (Persona 3, 4, 5, and Metaphor) during a Steam sale. My first attempt at Persona 1 on PSP went nowhere. A retro game with J2ME phone-level graphics has too high a barrier to entry for a newcomer, so I decided to try a game from a completely different franchise with an entirely different setting first.

My first impression was that the game asked us separately for the character's nickname and our own personal nickname. Unfortunately, it turned out this affects nothing except a couple of lines of dialogue.

### Technical Aspects

Performance also frequently leaves much to be desired. In some scenes, the Steam Deck at 720p delivers a full 60fps, and the 3090 at 4K hits 120fps. But in others, performance dropped below 30 on the Steam Deck and below 40 on the 3090. The game's graphics aren't exactly a masterpiece either. City scenes still look decent, but nature scenes are quite bad — almost PS3-level.

### Story (Introduction)

It all begins with the need to save a prince who was cursed by villains 7 years ago. We come from a sanctuary where his life is being sustained, to the capital city, where for some reason we enlist as army volunteers. The king is dead and no one knows what will happen next.

Inexplicable monsters called "humans" are breaking into the world. At the same time, one of the army's generals — Louis — is fighting them while also seeking to seize power in the country.

Once we become a recruit, we're sent to defend the border, where we must meet with an ally. But what happens there? Right — a "human" appears and causes a bloody massacre. Yet we don't just survive: we also gain a special power — the Archetype.

The entire combat system will ultimately be built around Archetypes, but to unlock new ones we'll need to meet many new people and strengthen our relationships with them. Relationships with characters grow through quest chains where we help them and get to know them better.

### Gameplay

The game is divided into separate episodes, each taking place in a new city. We have a limited number of days we can spend in the city before the next event. In a day we can typically take 2 actions — a daytime and a nighttime one — such as a companion quest, a dungeon run, or improving our reputation among the people.

The combat is standard for a JRPG — and compared to Expedition, even simpler: there are no QTEs.

### Impressions

I really enjoyed the game. Despite its flaws, it's very pleasant and warm, and I'll be remembering it for a long time.
