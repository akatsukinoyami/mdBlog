> [Post](https://t.me/katsu_diary/1440) from my channel

I have a very deep connection with the "Mushoku Tensei" franchise. I first encountered it during my first voyage as a cadet. Although we had a pretty friendly crew, sometimes I wanted to escape into my favorite books. 2016 — isekai weren't yet at the peak of their popularity and were quite high quality.

This story completely absorbed me. I really love stories that show realistic character development. I found something similar in the recently released anime ["How to Create Magic in Another World"](https://aniconnect.inkvi.us/59265), where the hero mastered the basics of previously non-existent magic, essentially creating it for the people of this world.

But let's return to "Reincarnation." The main character is a humiliated, beaten down, and driven to hermitage depressive "wizard" of 30+ years from his school days. It doesn't matter how he got to another world, but most importantly — he gets hope to start everything over. And not through the long path of crawling out of the pit he found himself in, but by literally resetting to zero. Isn't that a dream? <span class="opacity-0 hover:opacity-100 transform transition-opacity duration-1000 ease-in-out hover:duration-200">Shouldn't such isekai fall under articles about suicide popularization?</span>

A new world, a warm family, and an adult mind that allows you to start developing almost before you learn to walk. The best prerequisites for a happy life. But everything isn't that simple — this world is far from simple. The main character will become stronger and stronger, but he will actually develop, train, and invest his strength into it, rather than receive gifts out of nowhere. <span class="opacity-0 hover:opacity-100 transform transition-opacity duration-1000 ease-in-out hover:duration-200">Not counting the demon eye, but that's not so important))</span>

On one hand, Rudeus's life seems carefree, but it's not at all. He worries a lot about his loved ones from a very young age, and with each year, more and more of this responsibility falls on his shoulders.

<details><summary>Spoiler alert</summary>
The relationship between Rudeus and the biological father of his body — Paul Greyrat — seems particularly complex and therefore interesting. Paul himself is quite an ambiguous character: sometimes he seems like an exemplary family man, sometimes he cheats on his wife in the family home with a maid when she's pregnant, but then immediately rushes to search for his family members when trouble strikes. He's probably the most alive character in the series. Yes, he's a pervert, an alcoholic, and quite a bastard. But he was ready to die for his family members.

So, what I'm getting at... their relationship with Rudeus is also unusual for a father and son. First, he hits the hero for allegedly beating up a neighbor's child without understanding the situation, which shows his immaturity — after all, he was only 24-25 years old. But at the same time, he wanted to help Rudeus fulfill his dream and did everything he could for it, even pleading with nobles from a parallel branch of the aristocratic family.

At their next meeting, when Rudeus, being a ten-year-old boy, managed to cross the most dangerous continent on the planet, he got a scolding from his father, who hoped that the hero, as an adult person, would learn about the catastrophe and start looking for people. But Paul realizes this too, not without the help of friends.

And at the very end, Paul says goodbye to life to protect Rudeus from the attack of a huge Hydra.

Their relationship was very complex throughout the entire plot, but it was very real and made me feel it deeply.

</details>

I think many would like to get such a second chance to change their life, but unfortunately, this only happens in anime. So we need to try to live this life that we have, because there's no certainty that a harem of elf girls or catgirls awaits us later.
