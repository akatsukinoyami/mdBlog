Well, here we have reached the third part of my story.
In the last post, we passed the port of Shuweih in Kuwait, from which it was not so easy to get out. And in the end we reached the entrance to the port of Suets, for the passage of the canal of the same name.

_2020.10.01 02:40_ We were standing at the anchor in front of the entrance to Suets, and everything seemed to start well, but the fucking boatmen got twitchy.
There is a rule in the Suez Canal that a ship must take one or two boats, depending on the size, on board the ship to quickly moor it if it breaks down.
So, we went through with one boat all the time, but now they said either two boats or a tugboat for 34k bucks. As a result, they took off the whole brain and barely pushed the wounded boat with (t).
The shipowner also wrote that our ship will call at the port of Piraeus in Greece and there it will be transferred under another register (from the American Bureau of Shipping to the Korean Register), after which a detailed check of the PSC Paris MOU awaits us ... I don’t want anything anymore, just to be where something far...

_2020.10.01 14:00_ The rake task has come, after Piraeus we go to Ochakov and load on Aqaba in Jordan. Parking in Piraeus will take a week, walking to Aqaba - just over a month.

_2020.10.03 02.42 (UTC+3)_ We are sailing between the Greek Islands, which I do for the first time, towards the port of Piraeus. Tomorrow evening we should already anchor there, and later we will probably be taken to the port.
Today the sailors climbed into the ballast tanks together with the mechanics and found a new problem. It turned out that the pipe from the bilge wells (the place where the water from washing the cargo holds is stored) was broken and the pump pumped water from the ballast tank instead. But since the pipe there is covered in solid rust and on a bend, it is even impossible to weld it.
**UPD**: in the end, after p...th they were able to weld)
