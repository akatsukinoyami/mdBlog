In Russia, almost every day was a test for us.

On February 09, while cleaning a part in the engine room, our welder's cleaning disk broke in the grinder.
The goggles were knocked off by large pieces of the disk, and only the next day he felt that something had got into his eye.
His day could not be taken from the ship due to bad weather, and when they were taken, they took him all the way to Krasnodar to a large ophthalmological clinic.
So we became 23.

On February 10, the hydraulic cylinder of the covers on the fifth hold ruptured. Hydraulic oil spilled all over the deck, benefiting from a spilled load of wheat soaking it up. Luckily, there was a spare, albeit used, cylinder on the ship, so we were able to at least close this cover.

They say that in Russia there is a terrible bureaucracy and you need to run for certificates to get certificates ... They did not bypass the marine environment.

First, the customs should be cleared for departure, then the border guards, along with a bunch of documents, the agent sends them to the port supervision, and he issues a special paper, "Permission to Departure."
After that, you need to tell the individual number of this permission to another supervisor with the call sign "Kavkaz Radio 5", then, when they give the go-ahead, call "Kavkaz Traffic Control" and say that radio 5 gave the go-ahead and only now you can leave.

Now we are waiting for the mechanics to remove the cylinder cover, but... All three sets of special devices for this operation are broken!
