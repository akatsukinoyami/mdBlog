We left Odessa by bus towards Ust-Luga, a new port near St. Petersburg and traveled for about 40-44 hours, and on April 21, 2016, I ended up on the boat where I did my internship.
There were a lot of people 8-10 of us: the first mate, 3PKM, sailors, a cook and me.
Immediately upon arrival, I took over the watch at the gangway, as a cadet should.

_2016.05.10_ We approached the Suez Canal, it was my first passage through it. Instead of the usual external anchor, we were then placed inside the port, moored to the barrel and two anchors.

A barrel is a mooring device, which is a concrete anchor fixed in the ground with a float attached to the anchor by a chain.

Since the vessel was of the Heavy Lift class - that is, capable of carrying bulky cargo, it was equipped with its own cranes capable of lifting 150 tons. So taking mooring Suez boats was as easy as shelling pears.
We also had little problems, various locals tried to climb up to us to try to sell all kinds of game to the sailors.

In the photo below - the capture of a mooring boat and special quarantine lights, for not turning on which they took 9 blocks of cigarettes from us. In the residential superstructure, all entrances and exits were closed, leaving only one - near which we, the cadets, kept watch so that the local natives would not get inside.

Further, after a couple of days we got to the next Indian port of Hazira.

_2016.06.10_ Having left all the cargo in the previous ports, we arrived for loading. Since we still have a Heavy Lift, we were put on the delivery of 300 tons of generators in the UAE.
The process of loading such serious goods is not quite usual:
Firstly, there are two cranes in the park,
Secondly, a special high-speed ballast system is used to first tilt the ship towards the shore, secure the cargo, and then tilt the ship in the opposite direction, this is done because the force to lift the cargo off the ground is the largest and can damage even such powerful cranes.
